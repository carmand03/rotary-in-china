You are helping to build a text database from the archives of Rotary clubs in China and Manchuria (1919-1952). Each archival folder is a stack of separate documents scanned page after page: letters, membership rosters, semi-annual report forms, club bulletins, press clippings, certificates, binder cover sheets, tables of contents, and the secretariat's record cards. The database stores one record per document, so we need to know where each document starts.

You see two scanned pages from one folder, in order: first the PREVIOUS page, then the CURRENT page, each with a rough OCR excerpt. Decide how the CURRENT page relates to the previous one:

- `new`: the current page begins a different document.
- `continues`: the current page belongs to the same document as the previous page (the next page of a letter or bulletin issue, the reverse side of the same form, card or certificate).
- `blank`: the current page carries no content at all.

A document is one item produced by one creator at one time. Apply these rules for this collection:

- A cover sheet, a table of contents, a roster, a form, a letter, a bulletin issue, a certificate and a record card are each their own document, even when they follow one another and concern the same club.
- A letter that follows a form is a new document, even if it was sent with the form: look for a letterhead, a date line, a salutation.
- Each issue of a bulletin is one document covering all its pages; a new issue number or date in a masthead starts a new document.
- A multi-page roster or list is one document while the layout and numbering run on.
- The reverse side of a form, card or certificate continues it. A different card, even of the same series and colour, is a new document.

Evidence of continuation: running page numbers, a sentence carried across the page break, identical layout and typeface, a signature block closing a text that began on the previous page. Evidence of a new document: a letterhead, title or masthead, a date line, a salutation, a different printed form, a change of paper colour or size. Colour, size and layout are visible only in the images, so look at them; do not decide from the OCR alone.

First write `reason`: in one short sentence, what on the two pages shows it. Then give `relation`.
